# Security

The current protocol is pre-mainnet and unaudited. It requires an independent contract review, deployment rehearsal, and review of every production dependency before it can hold value.

## Enforced by the contracts

- `OTFToken` mints one billion tokens in its constructor and has no later mint, tax, blacklist, seizure, or upgrade path. Burns reduce live supply.
- Vault fee rates and beneficiaries are immutable. Fee shares use per-vault creator and buyback accounting recorded when each fee is charged.
- Vault accounting excludes token donations. A donation cannot improve backing in the ledger or change the OTF fee split.
- The collector can settle only factory-registered vaults through the configured entry router and approved adapters. Its final purchase uses the immutable canonical OTF/WETH V4 pool.
- Reward claims require a valid cumulative proof and always pay the user committed in the leaf. The root publisher cannot withdraw reward principal.
- Team-vesting beneficiary changes require nomination by the current beneficiary and acceptance by the nominee.
- The launch manager owns the permanent V4 position and exposes no liquidity removal, repricing, migration, or replacement function.

These controls constrain contract calls. They do not make external assets, venues, administrators, or price inputs safe.

## Trusted roles

### Reward-root publisher

The publisher chooses cumulative reward entitlements. A valid root can allocate the distributor's full remaining balance. The contract prevents forged proofs and duplicate claims, but it cannot detect an unfair allocation or a compromised publisher.

### Expense beneficiary

Each vault has one immutable expense beneficiary. Only that address can settle the vault's pending fee account. The beneficiary selects either a vault-share sale to WETH or a basket redemption followed by constituent sales, together with minimum WETH, minimum OTF, and a deadline.

Settlement uses exact token identities, approved adapters, minimum outputs, and observed balance changes. These checks limit malformed routes; they do not guarantee best execution. The protocol has no route discovery, oracle, TWAP, fair-value floor, or fixed slippage policy for fee settlement.

The collector clears the pending counters before external execution. It then consumes the exact pending share balance, pays the creator portion of actual WETH, spends only the buyback portion through the canonical OTF pool, and burns the OTF received. Any failure reverts the transaction and restores the pending state.

### Adapter manager and trading venues

The entry-router owner can approve or revoke adapters. An approved adapter must be immutably bound to the router, but its code and every external venue it calls remain trusted execution dependencies. Constituents, WETH, Permit2, Universal Router, and the selected V3 or V4 pools also lie outside the protocol's control.

Routes are supplied offchain. The contracts validate their shape and observed transfers but do not decide whether a route is economically favorable.

### ETH/USD oracle

Team vesting trusts the configured ETH/USD oracle. The vesting contract rejects nonpositive, future, or stale answers. A fresh but incorrect answer can still satisfy a vesting milestone.

The other input to vesting is the current canonical V4 spot price. The contract uses no TWAP, liquidity threshold, observation delay, or manipulation guard.

## Native ETH boundary

The entry router accepts native ETH only through `mintFromNative` and accepts callback ETH only from its immutable canonical WETH contract. Native mint requires exact value. Native redemption unwraps only WETH produced by the current operation and sends that amount atomically to the caller.

The router's reentrancy guard covers wrapping, unwrapping, refunds, and native sends. Rejected sends revert. Pre-existing or forcibly sent ETH and WETH remain untouched. Native identifiers never enter a vault, adapter leg, pool, or buyback route.

Application support for native basket execution remains disabled until a compatible router deployment is recorded.

## Launch and market assumptions

The canonical pool initializes at exactly 20 ETH reference FDV. Its fixed final tick represents `179.997388091 ETH`, the closest selected spacing-1 value to the 180 ETH target. Selling the full bootstrap range produces `8.999934702040754827 WETH` under the launch manager's integer math. At the final price, the 50 million OTF reserve has approximately the same WETH value.

The swap that reaches the final tick records `GraduationReady`. Anyone must then call `finalizeGraduation` in a separate transaction because V4 remains locked during the swap callback.

Zero LP fees do not remove transaction-ordering, slippage, liquidity, or price-impact risk. The launch manager's lack of administrative liquidity controls also means no recovery exists for the permanent position or residual dust.

## Vault accounting assumptions

Vaults track accounted balances rather than raw token balances. They assume each constituent and adapter behaves according to observed balance changes. Deflationary, rebasing, callback-enabled, or administratively mutable tokens may create backing deficits or block operations. Users must assess each constituent and venue.

Mint fees increase the gross backing required for a requested net share amount and round up. Redeem fees also round up. Creator portions of mint and redeem fees round down, while the remainder goes to buyback. Annual expense accounting carries a fractional split remainder across checkpoints so checkpoint timing does not change the cumulative allocation.

These rounding choices prevent unbacked shares and fee undercharging. A small operation may therefore require one additional raw unit.

## Deployment controls

Production deployment requires explicit WETH, PoolManager, StateView, PositionManager, Universal Router, Permit2, and ETH/USD oracle addresses. The repository provides no fallback production addresses.

The testnet configuration references an unofficial V4 mirror and pins expected runtime code hashes. A successful hash check identifies the reviewed runtime bytes at that address. It does not endorse the deployment or its operators.

The current architecture is not deployed. Earlier Robinhood testnet protocol addresses are incompatible and disabled in the application; there is no migration or legacy claim path.

## Production blockers

Before mainnet use, the project needs:

- an independent audit of the current contracts and economic arithmetic;
- fork and integration tests against the selected official production deployments;
- reviewed multisig, beneficiary, WETH, oracle, and adapter configuration;
- deployment and verification of the canonical-WETH entry router, including receiver and callback-reentrancy tests;
- deployed-bytecode checks and reconciliation of the complete one-billion-OTF allocation;
- operational procedures for route generation, minimum outputs, root publication, and artifact retention;
- browser and wallet tests against the final addresses.

Report vulnerabilities privately to the maintainers. Do not test public deployments with real user funds.
