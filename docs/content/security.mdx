# Security

The protocol is pre-mainnet. Mainnet use depends on completing the production checks below.

## Enforced by the contracts

- `OTFToken` mints one billion tokens in its constructor and has no later mint, tax, blacklist, seizure, or upgrade path. Burns reduce live supply.
- Vault fee rates and beneficiaries are immutable. The vault records the creator and buyback portions when it charges each fee.
- Token donations do not change the vault's accounted backing or OTF fee split.
- The collector can settle only factory-recognized vaults through the factory's canonical entry router and approved adapters. Its final purchase uses the immutable canonical OTF/WETH V4 pool.
- Reward claims require a valid cumulative proof and pay the user named in the leaf. The root publisher has no direct withdrawal function.
- Only the current team-vesting beneficiary can checkpoint or nominate and cancel a successor. The nominee must accept the role.
- The launch manager enforces the bootstrap price corridor, burns its final OTF balance once, and permanently locks its V4 liquidity position.

External assets, venues, administrators, and price inputs remain trust dependencies.

## Trusted roles

### Reward-root publisher

The publisher sets cumulative reward entitlements and must apply the [rewards policy](/token-and-fee-incentives#cumulative-rewards-and-incentive-emissions): 208 weeks, 650 million OTF for depositors, 50 million for creators, and a 10-million-OTF cap on each participant's weight.

The contract does not enforce this policy. A publisher can allocate the distributor's full remaining balance at any time. Proof verification and claim accounting prevent invalid proofs and duplicate claims, but cannot detect an incorrect or early allocation.

### Expense beneficiary

Only a vault's immutable expense beneficiary can settle its pending fees. The beneficiary chooses a vault-share sale to WETH or a basket redemption followed by constituent sales, and sets minimum WETH, minimum OTF, and a deadline.

The contracts check token identities, approved adapters, minimum outputs, and actual balance changes. The beneficiary remains responsible for execution quality: fee settlement has no route discovery, oracle, TWAP, fair-value floor, or fixed slippage policy.

The collector clears pending counters before external execution, consumes the recorded shares, pays the creator's portion of WETH, and uses the rest to buy and burn OTF through the canonical pool. Any failure reverts the transaction and restores pending fees.

### Adapter manager and trading venues

The entry-router owner can approve or revoke adapters. Each approved adapter must be immutably bound to the router. Its code and external venues remain trusted, as do constituent tokens, WETH, Permit2, Universal Router, and the selected V3 or V4 pools.

Offchain callers supply routes. The contracts validate route structure and transfers, but cannot determine whether a route offers a fair price.

### ETH/USD oracle

Team vesting uses the configured ETH/USD oracle and the current canonical V4 spot price. It rejects nonpositive, future, or stale oracle answers. A fresh but incorrect answer can still satisfy a milestone.

Only the current beneficiary can record a checkpoint. This requires trusting the beneficiary: the contract has no TWAP, liquidity threshold, observation delay, or guard against spot-price manipulation.

## Native ETH boundary

The entry router accepts user ETH through `mintFromNative`, with `msg.value` exactly matching the requested input. It accepts callback ETH only from its immutable WETH contract. Native redemption unwraps only the current operation's WETH output and sends that ETH to the caller.

Wrapping, unwrapping, refunds, and ETH sends stay inside the reentrancy guard. Rejected sends revert the transaction. Pre-existing or forcibly sent ETH and WETH remain untouched; vaults and swap routes use ERC-20 tokens only.

Native basket entry and exit are enabled on testnet.

## Launch and market assumptions

The `beforeAddLiquidity` hook reserves tick capacity for graduation. Before `Graduated`, it permits only the manager's bootstrap and permanent mints, checking the caller, pool, PositionManager, phase, ticks, and exact liquidity amount. Authorization exists only during each mint call.

External liquidity additions become available after graduation. External LPs cannot withdraw or transfer the manager's permanent position.

The pool starts at 15 ETH reference FDV and graduates at a fixed tick just below 135 ETH. Fixed bootstrap liquidity provides the WETH principal for a permanent position using at most 50 million OTF. The [security specification](/protocol-security-spec#canonical-launch) defines the exact boundaries, liquidity constants, and rounding requirements.

Swaps must finish inside the bootstrap boundaries. Reaching the exact final `sqrtPriceX96` sets `GraduationReady` and blocks further swaps until finalization. The launch router limits price at the boundary, partially fills oversized input, and normally finalizes in the same transaction after the PoolManager returns control. Anyone can also finalize separately once the boundary is reached.

Swaps through the standard router can succeed inside the corridor. An order that crosses a boundary reverts because that router does not expose the required price limit.

Finalization derives permanent liquidity amounts from pinned V4 math. Extra WETH stays locked; extra OTF joins the final burn. Any finalization failure also reverts a boundary purchase that invoked it. Zero LP fees do not remove transaction-ordering, slippage, liquidity, or price-impact risk.

## Vault accounting assumptions

Vaults track accounted balances. Deflationary, rebasing, callback-enabled, or administratively mutable tokens may create backing deficits or block operations. Users must assess each constituent and venue.

Mint fees increase the backing required for the requested net shares and round up. Redeem fees also round up. The creator's portion rounds down and the remainder goes to buyback. Annual expense splits carry fractional remainders across checkpoints so checkpoint timing does not change the cumulative allocation.

Rounding prevents unbacked shares and fee undercharging. Small operations may require one additional raw unit.

## Deployment controls

Production deployment requires explicit WETH, PoolManager, StateView, PositionManager, Universal Router, Permit2, and ETH/USD oracle addresses, with no fallback defaults.

Testnet uses an unofficial V4 mirror with pinned runtime hashes. A matching hash verifies the expected bytecode at an address; operators and dependencies still require review.

Run `corepack pnpm contracts:validate:mainnet-routing` to fork Robinhood mainnet at one recent block and check 23 pinned dependency runtimes. The suites cover launch graduation, basket and native entry/exit, mixed adapters, fee settlement, buyback burns, and slippage rollback. They also rehearse deployment and allocation, trade five real stock tokens, test in-kind exits after adapter revocation, and check vesting against the live ETH/USD feed.

The Solidity security workflow runs these tests on matching pushes and pull requests, and supports manual runs. The rehearsal allows a 90,000-second oracle age: the feed's 86,400-second heartbeat plus one hour. Final production roles, multisig execution, and the broadcast procedure remain unvalidated.

Current source uses the mainnet router's five-field V4 tuple. The [September 5 testnet deployment](/deployment-addresses) uses its venue's four-field tuple, includes the liquidity-capacity fix with hook mask `0x2840`, and clears Permit2 approvals. Its replacement adapters are approved and the previous V3 adapter is revoked. Fresh testnet deployment from current source is blocked until a compatible venue is reviewed and configured.

## Production blockers

Before mainnet use, the project needs:

- an independent audit of the current contracts and economic arithmetic;
- rehearsal of the final production deployment procedure with the selected roles and multisig;
- reviewed multisig, beneficiary, WETH, oracle, and adapter configuration;
- deployment and verification of the canonical-WETH entry router, including receiver and callback-reentrancy tests;
- deployed-bytecode checks and reconciliation of the complete one-billion-OTF allocation;
- operational procedures for route generation, minimum outputs, root publication, and artifact retention;
- browser and wallet tests against the final addresses.

Report vulnerabilities privately to the maintainers. Do not test public deployments with real user funds.
