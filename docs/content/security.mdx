# Security

The protocol is pre-mainnet and unaudited. The fee-settlement v3 contracts require a fresh review,
deployment rehearsal, and independent production configuration review before they hold value.

## Contract-enforced boundaries

- OTF is issued once at one billion tokens. No post-construction mint, tax, blacklist, seizure, or
  upgrade path exists. Holder burns reduce live supply.
- Fund fee rates and beneficiaries are immutable. All fee shares are held by the collector with
  separate per-vault creator and buyback accounting fixed when each fee is charged. Settlement
  checkpoints annual fees; shutdown exits remain fee-free.
- Raw constituent donations are excluded from fund accounting and the OTF fee-benefit calculation.
- The buyback collector can use only the protocol router's typed routes and approved adapters. Its
  final pool key is the immutable canonical OTF/WETH V4 pool. It has no asset withdrawal or arbitrary
  call path.
- The rewards distributor has no principal withdrawal or time gate. A valid proof can pay only its
  committed user and only the unclaimed cumulative amount.
- Permanent V4 liquidity remains owned by the launch manager, which exposes no removal, repricing,
  migration, or replacement function.

## Trusted roles

### Root-publishing multisig

The multisig is fully trusted to choose cumulative reward allocations. A valid root can allocate the
entire remaining rewards balance. The contract prevents proof forgery and double claims, not unfair
allocations or a compromised publisher.

### Expense beneficiaries and settlement routes

Only a vault's immutable expense beneficiary can settle that vault. The beneficiary chooses when to
claim and supplies either an approved fund-share sale ending in WETH or a typed basket-redemption
route to WETH, plus minimum WETH, minimum OTF, and deadline.
Exact token identities, adapter approval, minimum output, and observed balance-delta checks limit
mistakes and compromise; they do not guarantee optimal execution. The protocol deliberately has no
oracle, TWAP, route discovery, fair-value floor, or hardcoded slippage for this operation.

The collector clears both pending share counters before external execution, consumes their exact
combined share balance through the selected route, pays the creator share of actual WETH to the beneficiary, spends only the remaining buyback WETH in
the immutable canonical V4 pool, and burns all purchased OTF. Any failed step reverts the complete
transaction. Factory registration prevents fake vaults from creating or settling fee accounts.

### Adapter manager and external venues

The entry/exit router owner can approve or revoke adapters. Approved adapter code, V3/V4 venue code,
constituent tokens, WETH, Permit2, and Universal Router are external trust boundaries. Routes are not
discovered onchain.

The entry/exit router accepts native ETH only through its explicit guarded endpoints and only accepts
callback ETH from its immutable canonical WETH. Native mint requires exact value; native redemption
unwraps and sends only the transient WETH output. Checked sends revert atomically when a receiver
rejects ETH. Pre-existing or forcibly sent ETH/WETH must remain untouched, and no native marker may
reach an adapter leg. Native basket execution remains disabled in the application until a compatible
router is deployed and configured.

### ETH/USD oracle

The production oracle is trusted for team vesting. The contract rejects nonpositive, future, or
stale answers, but a fresh incorrect answer can unlock a milestone. The V4 price is intentionally the
current spot price in a potentially thin pool; no TWAP, liquidity threshold, delay, or manipulation
protection is provided.

## Market and launch assumptions

The pool initializes at exactly 10 ETH launch reference FDV; its one-sided position boundary and
approximately 90 ETH final price use deterministic discrete ticks. The final tick marks graduation
in its swap callback; a separate permissionless
transaction finalizes positions because V4 remains locked during the callback. Transaction ordering,
slippage, and pool price impact remain market risks even though the LP fee is 0%.

The launch manager locks principal by omitting every administrative liquidity-control path. That
also means no recovery exists for permanent liquidity or deterministic residual dust.

## Fund accounting assumptions

Vaults use accounted balances rather than raw balances. They assume constituent token transfers and
approved adapters behave according to observed balance deltas. Deflationary, rebasing, callback, or
administratively mutable assets may create deficit or liveness risks. Users must evaluate every
constituent and venue independently.

Mint fees calculate the gross backing required for the requested net investor shares and round up.
Redeem fees round up and redeem the remaining net shares. Creator split basis points and
mint/redeem creator fee shares round down; the residual goes to buyback. Annual expense splitting
carries a fractional remainder across checkpoints so cadence does not change cumulative allocation.
These directions avoid unbacked shares and fee undercharging but can make small operations pay one
raw unit more.

## Deployment controls

Production WETH, PoolManager, StateView, PositionManager, Universal Router, Permit2, and ETH/USD
oracle addresses are mandatory inputs. No production address is embedded as a fallback. Testnet uses
an unofficial V4 mirror only after configured runtime bytecode hashes match. A hash match verifies
the reviewed bytes at that address; it is not an endorsement of the mirror or its operational
security.

The old testnet deployment is incompatible and disabled in the application. No migration or legacy
claim path exists.

## Production blockers

- independent audit of all v3 contracts and economic arithmetic;
- fork/integration tests against the exact official production V4 deployments;
- verified production multisig, beneficiary, WETH, and oracle selections;
- deployment and verification of the canonical-WETH native entry/exit router plus receiver and
  callback-reentrancy integration tests;
- deployed-code verification and complete one-billion-token allocation reconciliation;
- operational procedures for route generation, minimum outputs, root publication, and artifact
  availability;
- frontend browser and wallet testing against the final deployed addresses.

Report vulnerabilities privately to the maintainers. Do not test against public deployments with
real user funds.
